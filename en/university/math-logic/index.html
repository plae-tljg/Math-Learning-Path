Mathematical Logic

Mathematical logic corresponds to the Second Principle: mechanical deduction. When proving, you can temporarily ignore content and just follow the form.

The Core of Logic: Form Itself

The core of logic is form itself. Given a string of symbols, it transforms symbols according to rules without caring what the symbols represent. For example, Modus Ponens: from "if $P$ then $Q$" and "$P$", directly infer "$Q$".

Spatial vs Temporal Nature

Spatial

Commutative law: $P \land Q \equiv Q \land P$ - two propositions side by side, order doesn't matter.

Temporal

Transitivity of implication: $P \to Q, Q \to R \Rightarrow P \to R$ - clearly a chain of steps, a path from start to finish.
